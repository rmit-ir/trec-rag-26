 - RIGHT VS. PRIVILEGE: POLICY IMPLICATIONS
 - The U.S. Constitution contains no explicit right to healthcare, though scholars argue the 14th Amendment's Due Process and Equal Protection Clauses create implied access regardless of financial circumstances.
 - A legal right is constitutionally protected and cannot be taken away, while a privilege is granted or withheld at government discretion.
 - Treating healthcare as a right shifts policy from market-based access toward government duty to ensure coverage, creating enforceable entitlements rather than aspirational principles.
 - Unlike natural rights such as life and liberty, healthcare would become a legal right that is "potentially the most costly right of all," requiring government to provide an acceptable level of care for every citizen.
 - UNIVERSAL COVERAGE AND INTERNATIONAL STANDARDS
 - The United States is the only wealthy, industrialized nation without a universal healthcare system.
 - Among 11 high-income nations, the U.S. ranks last in health system performance on access, efficiency, and equity, despite spending the highest per capita among OECD nations.
 - U.S. health outcomes are worst among high-income nations: lowest life expectancy, highest death rates for avoidable conditions, and highest maternal and infant mortality.
