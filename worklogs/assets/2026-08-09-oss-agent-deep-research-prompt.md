# Deep-research request: closing the open-weight quality gap in a TREC RAG 2026 agentic RAG system

You are advising on an evaluated, working research system, not designing one from scratch. Sections 1–5 are empirical ground truth from a real, live system with real judged scores as of **2026-08-09** — treat them as fixed observation, not something to re-derive. Where you disagree with or want to complicate a finding, say so explicitly and state what new evidence would resolve the disagreement, rather than silently ignoring it. Section 6 is your task.

## 1. Task, constraints, and evaluation protocol

- **Track**: TREC RAG 2026, Research Report track. Given a narrative request, retrieve from a fixed corpus (ClimbMix — no web search, no external knowledge) and produce a long-form, cited answer.
- **Answer format**: a `references[]` docid list; every sentence has ≤3 citation indices; whole answer ≤1024 words.
- **Official organizer evaluation, two independent axes**: (a) blind pairwise system-vs-system arena (randomized presentation order), and (b) per-topic nugget/rubric grading against narrative-specific criteria, plus a separately weighted citation precision/recall score computed by the organizers (not something we compute ourselves — see below). These two axes can rank the same two configurations in **opposite order** (documented in §3) — any proposal should say which axis it targets, ideally both.
- **Our own internal proxy metric** (used for every number below, NOT the same as the organizers' own final scoring, but built to mirror the official rubric criteria as closely as possible for cheap internal iteration): one LLM judge call per (system, topic) grades every official rubric criterion for that topic 0–2, grouped into six axes (Communication Quality, Explicit Criteria, Implicit Criteria, Instruction Following, References & Citation Quality, Synthesis of Information — plus a small "Miscellaneous" bucket), *and* separately assigns one holistic `overall` score, 0–3, for the whole answer. **`overall` is graded directly by the judge as a single integer per topic, not derived arithmetically from the six axis scores** — the two are correlated but not the same number. All "standalone overall" figures below are the mean of that 0–3 integer across topics; all "axis mean" figures are the mean of that axis's individual 0–2 criterion grades pooled across every criterion instance scored for that run (not one number per topic — e.g. "References & Citation Quality, n=9" means 9 individual criterion instances were graded across all 15 topics for that axis, so a single bad/good instance moves it a lot). **The judge model is `gpt-5.6-terra`, a proprietary OpenAI model** — this is evaluation infrastructure, run identically across every system in this document (open-weight or proprietary), never a component of any submitted system's own generation pipeline. Judging is explicitly OUT of scope for the open-weight constraint below.
- **Noise floor**: with 15 topics, one topic's grade flipping by one point moves the mean `overall` by exactly 1/15 ≈ 0.067. This was empirically confirmed once (an identical config regenerated and rescored moved by exactly 0.067). Treat any reported delta at or below 0.067 as noise, not a confirmed effect, unless independently replicated on a second topic set.
- **A failed-to-complete topic is EXCLUDED from the mean, not zero-filled.** E.g. `kimi-k2-thinking`'s 1.071/3 (§4) is the mean over the 14 topics it actually finished, not scored as 0 on the 15th — so it is not directly comparable to a 15/15 run's mean on completion-rate grounds; always read a reliability/completion-rate figure alongside any score for that reason.
- **Hard constraint on the system this request is about (`oss_agent`)**: every model call inside the GENERATION pipeline — main writer, brief/planning call, blind-scout call, review/critique call, any future citation-verifier or reranker that materially shapes what gets written or cited — must run on an **open-weight** model. In practice this project has only deployed via **AWS Bedrock**; a non-Bedrock open-weight route (self-hosted, another cloud's managed endpoint) is not disqualified in principle but has not been used or tested. A purely mechanical, non-generative component (e.g. a classical BM25/Lucene retrieval step, a deterministic citation-index mapper) is not "a model call" in this sense and is unconstrained. A small discriminative neural reranker or NLI/entailment model used only to REORDER or FLAG evidence (never to generate text that reaches the final answer) is a gray area we have not needed to rule on yet — if you propose one, say explicitly whether you intend it to need open weights too, and why.
  - Confirmed open-weight Bedrock models under our account **as of 2026-08-09**: `openai.gpt-oss-120b-1:0` (~120B MoE, works in two regions), `qwen.qwen3-next-80b-a3b` (~80B MoE, one region pair only), `moonshot.kimi-k2-thinking` (a reasoning/"thinking" model, one region pair only). All three support the harness's native tool-calling protocol (§2) without modification. No Llama, Mistral, or DeepSeek model is currently wired in. **Open question for you**: what other open-weight models are available on Bedrock (or an equally practical hosted route) as of your knowledge cutoff, and do any of them plausibly close a meaningful fraction of the gap in §4? For any model you recommend, state its approximate parameter count/architecture, context length, whether it supports function/tool calling, and your confidence that it is actually deployable via Bedrock (vs. only available elsewhere) — we cannot verify live availability ourselves as part of this request.
- **Budget reality**: open-weight Bedrock inference is roughly **8–10x cheaper per topic** than the proprietary models this system's shared architecture was originally designed around (§4 gives an exact figure). A full 15-topic generation-plus-internal-judging pilot on an open-weight model costs low single-digit dollars. We have roughly **$150–200 of remaining budget** for a next round of work along these lines — real headroom to spend on more inference (bigger ensembles, more rounds, heavier prompts, multi-pass drafting) if that turns out to be the right lever, not the binding constraint here.

## 2. Shared system architecture already available to build on

The whole project is built on one shared **staged-context, continuous tool-calling agent loop** (one long conversation with a single model). The model repeatedly calls `search` (choosing an engine and a query), `get_documents` (full-text fetch by id), and `commit_context` (curate the currently "staged" — visible-for-one-round — evidence into a persistent, growing context it writes from). Uncommitted staged evidence expires after one round. The final cited-prose report is produced as a normal turn in the same conversation, then deterministically mapped from inline citations to a `references[]` list — there is no separate "writer" call distinct from the loop.

**Retrieval engines already implemented and independently selectable per search call:**

| Engine | Mechanism |
|---|---|
| `semantic` | dense embedding match — conceptual/broad-topic queries |
| `keyword` | hosted BM25, bag-of-words OR (Boolean operators ignored) — exact names/ids/strings |
| `hybrid` | dense+sparse fused with Reciprocal Rank Fusion — general-purpose default, ~2x the cost of one engine call |
| `ssr` | Cottontail Shortest-Substring Ranking over a Boolean query language (GCL) — required terms, phrases, proximity; a required-but-absent term returns a truthful empty set instead of a near-miss |
| `lucene_bool` | full Lucene query-parser (Boolean AND/OR, required `+`/excluded `-` terms, phrases, proximity), BM25-ranked |

**Pluggable mechanisms the shared harness already supports as parameters to the same underlying `run_agent` call — proven, working code, not hypothetical:**
- A pre-flight, tool-less LLM call whose output is appended as advisory text to the system prompt before the main loop starts (used today for both a "requirements brief" and a "blind obligation scout" — §3/§4).
- Zero-LLM-call search-result augmentation (currently: auto-fetching the page immediately before/after each top hit).
- Post-search relevance filtering (narrow or annotate a batch before it's staged).
- A `judge_relevance` side-tool: the model may, at its own judgment, spend one extra cheap-model call mid-loop to check whether a doubtful batch actually supports a named requirement.
- Positional or LLM-generated previews/snippets that cap how much of each staged result's text the model sees (a token-cost lever — see §3 for why this was rejected).
- A `pre_final_hook` that fires exactly once, right before a valid final report is accepted, and can send the model back for exactly one revision with structured feedback (the "review/revise pass" below).
- An optional `release` field on commits, letting the model retroactively drop an earlier-committed document a better one supersedes.

**Other systems in the same codebase, for comparison** (same corpus/tools; "proprietary" = uses closed `gpt-5.6-*` OpenAI models):
- `aus_agent` — the original tuned-prompt baseline (proprietary).
- `aus_agent_v2` (**proprietary**) — this project's single best-scoring system: an isolated coverage plan, a second *blind* obligation-scout call (sees only the raw request, never the first plan, independently recalls additional atomic checks — this is the one mechanism ported into `oss_agent`, §4), the same staged search/commit loop, deterministic citation mapping. Ships several off-by-default experimental extras that never beat this lean default on a full run: observable-unit scouting, priority reconciliation, post-draft coverage verification/repair, a fully typed atomic coverage-contract/ledger replacing free-text commits, and an extractive `candidate_union` multi-draft selector (§3 has the relevant offline-audit numbers).
- `facets_agent` (proprietary) — a much shorter (~50-line), minimal-prompt configuration of the same harness. Scores lower than the tuned-prompt systems.
- `facet_rag` (proprietary) — a different shape: a scripted plan, then a per-facet orchestrator/analyzer/curator loop, then a separate synthesis pass. The weakest system with real generated output in the project.
- `brief_revise_agent` (proprietary) — a pre-flight requirements-brief call plus one review/revise pass over the same shared loop. Ties `aus_agent_v2` on our internal rubric grading at roughly 1/50th its generation cost.
- `oss_agent` (**open-weight only, the system this request is about**) — takes `brief_revise_agent`'s architecture as its base and adds `aus_agent_v2`'s blind-scout stage, restricted to open-weight models in every role (§4).

## 3. Full factor taxonomy already tested (proprietary models, unless marked open-weight)

| ID | Factor | Mechanism | Result |
|---|---|---|---|
| S1 | Requirements brief | one pre-flight call → ≤8 requirements appended to system prompt | always on; foundational, not toggled |
| S4 | Review/revise pass | one reviewer call + ≤1 revision turn before accepting the final report | always on for the systems that have it |
| S5 | Adjacent-page augmentation | zero-LLM-call ±1-page fetch around top hits | **real, confirmed effect** — off costs −0.067 to −0.133 vs. on (proprietary); **−0.400 for open-weight `qwen`** (§4, the largest ablation effect measured in the whole project) |

Not adopted anywhere in the project as a result. |
| S10 | Stage search results at all | vs. only allowing explicit full-text reads | negative |
| S11 | `judge_relevance` side-tool | model-initiated relevance side-check | null |
| S12 | `commit_release` | let the model retract a superseded committed doc | null (in 15 real runs, never once invoked) |
| S13 | Mandatory requirement-labelled retrieval screener | historical, reverted | measured worse, removed from the codebase entirely |
| S14 | **Blind obligation scout** (added 2026-08-09, ported unmodified from `aus_agent_v2`) | second independent pre-flight call | see §4 — first isolated A/B result for this factor anywhere in the project, run on an open-weight model |
| M1 | Main writer model | which model runs the loop | **by far the dominant standalone-score factor** — effect range −0.40 to −1.07 vs. the best proprietary model, roughly 10x any structural lever tested (proprietary models only; §4 extends this to open-weight models) |
| M2 | Requirements-brief-analyst model | which model writes the brief, main model held fixed | null — identical effect (−0.067) across three different analyst models tried, with a **proprietary** main model. | M3 | Reviewer model | which model reviews | never varied |

**Once the generator model is fixed, structural/harness toggles move the standalone score by less than one topic-grade almost every time** — 10 of 11 post-model-selection moves in the original proprietary-model factor sweep landed at or below the ±0.067 noise floor.

**An offline three-repeat frozen-judgment audit** (not a live scored run; an internal 0–1 scale, not the 0–3 scale used elsewhere in this document) inside `aus_agent_v2`'s own development estimated a diversity ceiling across 4–8 independently generated complete drafts of the same topics: an **oracle** whole-answer selector (picking, with perfect hindsight of the judged grades, the single best of 4 complete drafts) reached 0.7445; an **oracle** criterion-by-criterion union of the same 4 drafts (again with perfect hindsight, assembling the best-graded content per criterion across drafts) reached 0.8048; all 8 fully-agentic drafts pooled reached 0.8284. **These are oracle upper bounds computed with foreknowledge of the judged grades, not achieved by any real selector or merger** — they show real information is distributed across independent drafts that no single draft captures, but do not by themselves prove an implementable LLM-based selector/merger can realize that gain. A live best-of-4 ensemble probe (reuse the base config's own answer as candidate A, 3 fresh independent same-config reruns as B/C/D, one real selector call per topic, no oracle) on the strongest proprietary configuration returned a **clean null**: the selected output tied a single unselected fresh rerun exactly — i.e. at that sampling configuration there was no real diversity across reruns for a real selector to find and exploit. Whether a genuinely diversity-inducing multi-draft mechanism (e.g. different retrieval strategies or criterion partitions per draft, not just resampling the same config) could realize more of the oracle gap remains untested.

**Standalone rubric score and pairwise arena preference can rank the same two configurations in opposite order.** Clearest case: the `hybrid`-only engine change (S6) looked like a strict win on standalone grading (higher score, replicated twice, lower cost) but reversed to a clear arena loss when checked head-to-head against the same opponent the base configuration was already losing to. **Do not let any proposal graduate to "adopt" on a single positive standalone-score signal** — this project has one clean, documented case of exactly that going wrong.

## 4. `oss_agent`: this session's new open-weight evidence

Same 15-topic development subset used throughout §3, same internal judge protocol, so every number below is directly comparable to §3's.

**Three-way bake-off**, each model run as the full architecture's main writer, with brief/scout/review roles ALSO set to the same open-weight model (i.e. every role in a given bake-off cell is one single open-weight model, not mixed):

| Model | Topics completed | Standalone overall (0–3) | Reliability notes |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| `qwen.qwen3-next-80b-a3b` | 15/15 | **1.200** | clean on the first attempt, default round cap |
| `openai.gpt-oss-120b-1:0` | 15/15 | 1.200 (tied) | needed the harness's round cap raised from 30→100 (at 30, only 3/15 topics finished before hitting the ceiling), plus one retry for a transient tool-call-id `KeyError` in the shared harness (not `oss_agent`'s own code — see §5) |
| `moonshot.kimi-k2-thinking` | 14/15 | 1.071 (excludes the 1 incomplete topic, not zero-filled) | one topic reproducibly failed/hung across three separate attempts: a `KeyError` on the first try, then two timeouts on retries at 40–60 rounds |

**Every open-weight configuration tested scores far below every proprietary configuration in §3** (1.07–1.20 here vs. 1.87–2.27 for the proprietary systems named in §5's own comparison table below, identical judge, identical topics, identical 0–3 scale).

**A real, already-collected data point on role-decoupling** (relevant to M2 above): a prior proprietary-era experiment ran `qwen.qwen3-next-80b-a3b` as ONLY the main writer inside `brief_revise_agent`, with the brief-analyst and reviewer roles left on the proprietary `gpt-5.6-luna` model (i.e. a mixed pipeline, not open-weight end-to-end, run id `br-model-main-qwen-exp15-b1`): **1.400/3**. `oss_agent`'s fully-open-weight qwen configuration (brief, scout, and reviewer also all `qwen`) scores **1.200/3** — a **−0.200** difference attributable to open-weighting the supporting roles too, on top of the main-writer swap. This suggests M2's "brief-analyst model choice doesn't matter" finding (established with a proprietary main model) may NOT hold once the main model itself is weaker — the supporting roles' own capability may matter more when the writer they're supporting is already weaker. This has not been isolated further (we don't yet know whether the −0.200 comes from the brief, the reviewer, or both).

**Ablations** (qwen, same 15 topics, against the 1.200 default above):

- Disabling the blind scout (S14): **ties exactly** on the holistic score (1.200 either way, n=15 topics both) — but `Implicit Criteria`'s axis mean moves from 0.694 (scout on, n=170 criterion instances) to 0.571 (off, n=170) — the exact axis the mechanism targets. Kept on.
- Disabling adjacent-page augmentation (S5): drops the score to **0.800** (Δ = −0.400, n=15 both) — the single largest ablation effect measured anywhere in this project on either model family. Not a close call.

**Per-axis breakdown, `oss_agent` (qwen default) vs. the two proprietary baselines it's built from** (same 15 topics, same judge, `n` = pooled criterion instances scored for that axis that run — small n on some axes, read with appropriate caution):

| Axis (0–2 scale) | `oss_agent` qwen (n) | `brief_revise_agent` sol baseline (n) | `aus_agent_v2` (n) | Gap: sol − qwen |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Communication Quality | 0.633 (30) | 0.733 (30) | 0.833 (30) | **+0.100** |
| Explicit Criteria | 1.239 (109) | 1.450 (109) | 1.505 (109) | **+0.211** |
| **Implicit Criteria** | 0.694 (170) | 0.988 (170) | 1.100 (170) | **+0.294 (largest gap)** |
| Instruction Following | 0.875 (16) | 1.000 (16) | 1.188 (16) | +0.125 |
| **Synthesis of Information** | 0.590 (39) | 0.795 (39) | 1.000 (39) | **+0.205 (2nd-largest gap)** |
| References & Citation Quality | 0.667 (9) | 0.222 (9) | 0.000 (9) | **−0.444 (qwen scores HIGHER here)** |
| `overall` (0–3, holistic, not derived from the above) | **1.200** | 2.267 | 2.267 | +1.067 |

**This directly contradicts a naive reading of §3's project-wide finding that References & Citation Quality is universally the weakest axis** — that finding is real in aggregate across dozens of proprietary-model cells (§3, project-wide mean 0.167/2), but for THIS specific gap (open-weight `oss_agent` vs. its two proprietary progenitors), citation quality is not a weak point at all — it's `oss_agent`'s single best-scoring axis, and actually beats both proprietary baselines on it (n=9 is small — worth confirming on a larger sample, but a −0.444 gap in the FAVORABLE direction is a large, specific, surprising signal, not noise). **The two axes with the largest deficits for `oss_agent` specifically are Implicit Criteria and Synthesis of Information** — obligations a careful reader would infer but the request doesn't state outright, and turning gathered evidence into a coherent, complete piece of prose, respectively. The already-kept-on S14 scout targets Implicit Criteria directly and shows a real (if `overall`-score-invisible) effect there (above). Nothing in this project has yet specifically targeted Synthesis of Information.

**Cost**: the qwen default configuration runs at roughly **$0.16/topic** (real metered Bedrock rate) vs. **$1.29/topic** for the cheapest full proprietary configuration in §3 (`brief_revise_agent`'s own sol base cell) — about 8x cheaper.

## 5. What has explicitly NOT been tried for an open-weight main model, and what was checked in project history and rejected

**Checked against project history and explicitly NOT re-tried** (please don't re-propose without a genuinely new angle): the LLM-generated-snippet cost/quality tradeoff (S9), `judge_relevance`/`commit_release`/wider engine sets/positional preview (all null or negative, S9/S11/S12), and the historical mandatory requirement-labelled retrieval screener (S13, reverted).

**Not yet tried for an open-weight main model:**
- `ssr` / `lucene_bool` (precise Boolean/phrase retrieval) alone or combined with `hybrid` — every retrieval-engine experiment to date, proprietary or open-weight, has only used `semantic`/`keyword`/`hybrid`.
- Any dedicated re-ranking of retrieved candidates before staging (cross-encoder or otherwise) — nothing like this exists anywhere in the codebase.
- A dedicated citation-verification/entailment pass that re-reads each cited source and confirms the SPECIFIC claim it's attached to actually appears there. A related mechanism (an "UNSUPPORTED_CLAIM"/"CONTRADICTION" extension to the review pass) was tried once on a proprietary model already scoring 2.267/3 with a strong citation baseline and returned a null result — **given §4's own finding that citation quality is NOT `oss_agent`'s weak axis, this may be a lower-priority direction for `oss_agent` specifically than it would first appear**, unless you have reason to weight it differently.
- Further isolating the −0.200 role-decoupling gap in §4 (is it the brief, the scout, the reviewer, or the interaction of all three that needs a stronger open-weight model, if any?).
- Spending the ~8–10x cost headroom open-weight models provide on genuinely heavier inference: more search/commit rounds, more than the current single revision pass, or actually building a realizable (non-oracle) version of the multi-draft criterion-union mechanism §3's offline audit found real but unexploited headroom in.
- A system prompt written for a weaker open-weight model's own instruction-following profile, rather than reusing the tuned prompt built against the proprietary model family verbatim. Direction unknown: a much shorter minimal prompt scored LOWER than the tuned prompt for proprietary models (`facets_agent`, §2), but that comparison has never been run with an open-weight generator, where the right amount of explicit scaffolding could go either way.
- No experiment has separated "the open-weight model retrieves/curates worse evidence" from "the open-weight model writes worse prose from the same evidence" — e.g. replaying a strong proprietary system's own already-committed evidence set unchanged and having the open-weight model write ONLY from that fixed pool, to see how much of the ~1.07-point `overall` gap (concentrated in Implicit Criteria and Synthesis per §4's table) is retrieval/strategy vs. synthesis/writing.
- Whether other open-weight models exist on Bedrock (or an equally practical hosted route) beyond the three already tried (§1).

**Known unknowns we cannot currently answer ourselves** (flag your recommendations that would require these rather than assuming an answer): full per-query search-log statistics (empty-result rate, query length/quality, engine-choice distribution) broken out by model; a sentence-level or claim-level audit of exactly WHY implicit criteria or synthesis-quality grades are low (vague prose? missing content entirely? content present but not well organized?); the organizers' own official citation precision/recall figures for any of these systems (we only have our own internal rubric-axis proxy); repeat-seed variance for any single cell (we have one documented same-config rerun, moving by exactly one topic-grade, §1, but no systematic seed-variance study).
